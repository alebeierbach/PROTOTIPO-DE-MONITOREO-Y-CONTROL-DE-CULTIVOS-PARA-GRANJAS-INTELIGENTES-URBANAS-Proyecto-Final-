\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
This Integrative Project describes the design, construction, and experimental validation of a smart vertical urban farm prototype, developed within the framework of the LIADE (Laboratory of Applied Research and Electronic Development) research line on urban and peri-urban agriculture. The system's objective is to automate indoor cultivation by autonomously monitoring and controlling the main environmental variables --temperature, air humidity, soil moisture, and light intensity-- without requiring specialized knowledge from the end user. A modular, multi-level physical structure was designed and built for soil-based cultivation, using arugula and spinach as model crops selected for their agronomic adaptability to indoor conditions. A Raspberry Pi 4 single-board computer was selected as the central processing unit, receiving digital and analog sensor readings and driving the actuators through solid-state and electromechanical relay stages: full-spectrum LED lighting panels, a localized drip irrigation network with water recovery and recirculation, and forced ventilation. Custom parts designed through additive manufacturing solved waterproofing, drainage, and mechanical mounting challenges throughout the structure. On the software side, a modular control logic --based on threshold and hysteresis criteria, signal filtering, and sensor redundancy-- was implemented in Python to sustain the microclimate and irrigation within the target parameters at all times, while an accumulated-budget algorithm regulates the artificial photoperiod according to the daily light integral (DLI) received by each crop. The prototype also integrates cloud telemetry and a remote web interface, enabling continuous monitoring and configuration of the system in line with the Internet of Things (IoT) paradigm. Experimental trials conducted over several months validated the system's ability to sustain a stable and favorable microclimate for crop development, while power consumption measurements confirmed that the prototype operates within an energy-efficient range comparable to that of a standard desktop computer.
